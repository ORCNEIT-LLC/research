# ORCNEIT community code of conduct

[Русский](../../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) · [Contributing](contributing.md)

ORCNEIT brings together people who want to understand research, improve tools and help develop language technologies. Different experience and disagreement are useful; humiliation, threats and coercion are not. Participants do not have to be professional developers, researchers or fluent English speakers to be treated with respect.

## Scope

These rules apply to discussions, Issues, pull requests, reviews and other community spaces managed by ORCNEIT, as well as interactions on behalf of the community. They apply to project maintainers too.

Company services, including Contributors, retain their own mandatory documents and procedures. This code does not replace service terms, material-transfer rules or account-restriction appeals. Nor does it make every participant's private activity a representation of ORCNEIT.

## How we interact

- Critique a proposal, method or result, not a person's worth. Explain the disagreement and the evidence that could change the conclusion.
- Ask clarifying questions, share sources and help newcomers without mocking their experience or language.
- Distinguish measurements, assumptions and opinions. Do not present an unverified capability as a ready feature or a limited experiment as proof of superiority.
- Respect boundaries: declined private contact, requests to stop contact and decisions not to disclose personal information.
- Correct your own mistakes, acknowledge others' contributions and consider reasonable feedback.

Respect does not require agreement. You may directly identify errors, negative results and weak evidence. Declining a proposal or criticizing a conclusion is not itself a violation.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Threats, stalking, bullying, sexual harassment and degrading or discriminatory statements based on personal characteristics, including origin, nationality, gender, age, health, religion and identity.
- Personal insults, systematic provocation and continued unwanted private contact after a request to stop.
- Publishing others' personal data, private correspondence or sensitive information without a proper basis, or threatening disclosure.
- Impersonating another participant or an ORCNEIT representative, fabricating results, taking credit for others' work or deliberately misleading the community.
- Spam, mass duplicate messages, intrusive advertising and organized disruption of discussions.
- Publishing active credentials, exploiting a vulnerability to access others' data, and retaliating against a reporter, witness or anyone who raises a concern in good faith.

A technical or research context does not justify this behavior. When describing disputed material, include only what is necessary, warn about sensitive content and do not republish prohibited data as part of the discussion.

## Handling disagreements

Where possible, bring the conversation back to the specific question: what happened, what result is expected and what evidence is available. A pause or a maintainer's help in defining the scope of discussion can be useful.

You are not required to confront someone who threatens, harasses or violates your boundaries. You can report immediately; attempted reconciliation is not a prerequisite for a review.

## Reporting a violation

Contact [report@orcneitlab.com](mailto:report@orcneitlab.com) privately. Do not open a public issue containing personal information, private correspondence or allegations that reveal a reporter's identity.

Where possible, include:

- a link to the discussion or location of the incident;
- a brief account of events and approximate time;
- messages or screenshots necessary to understand the situation, without unnecessary personal data;
- whether the behavior is continuing or there is an immediate threat;
- a way to contact you if clarification is needed.

Do not send passwords, access keys or large exports of other people's data. For a possible vulnerability, a safe description and minimal example are enough: do not continue testing beyond authorized access.

If the report concerns someone involved in handling it, say so. That person should not be the sole decision-maker on a complaint about their own conduct. If reconsideration or another contact channel is needed, write to [contact@orcneitlab.com](mailto:contact@orcneitlab.com), referencing the earlier correspondence.

## How reports are handled

ORCNEIT considers the facts, context, severity and risk of continued harm. Clarification and participants' explanations may be needed. Where there is an immediate threat, access to a discussion may be restricted before review is complete.

Possible responses include explaining the rules, requiring the behavior to stop, hiding a violating message, limiting a discussion, issuing a warning, temporarily restricting participation or excluding someone from an ORCNEIT-managed community space. The response should fit the circumstances; this list does not require every step to be followed in every case.

Reports are accessible only to those who need them for review and protective action. Absolute anonymity is not promised: circumstances may identify a participant, and the law may require disclosure. Personal data and unnecessary complaint details are not published as part of a public dispute.

If a decision concerns you, you may request an explanation and reconsideration, supplying new facts or identifying an error, at [report@orcneitlab.com](mailto:report@orcneitlab.com). Public harassment and pressure are not appeal mechanisms. Account and service procedures follow their separate rules.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers should apply these rules consistently, distinguish technical disagreement from misconduct and not use moderation to suppress good-faith criticism. Project access does not exempt anyone from these responsibilities. The community must not require a reporter to prove a complaint publicly or tolerate retaliation.

This code defines community participation; it does not guarantee proposal acceptance, a project invitation, employment or compensation. Repository-specific rules can clarify the process but cannot remove the requirement to treat participants respectfully.
